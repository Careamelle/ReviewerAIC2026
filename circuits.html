DC Circuits (Elec 03-01 to 03-06)

1. If a resistance to which a constant voltage is applied is halved, what power dissipation will result?
Answer: A. Doubled
Rationale: Power is calculated as P = V² / R. If the resistance R is halved (R/2), the formula becomes V² / (R/2) = 2(V² / R), which means power is doubled.
2. If the reference point is changed to the earth point then any voltage taken w.r.t. the earth is known as
Answer: A. Absolute potential
Rationale: Voltages measured relative to a true earth ground are absolute potentials, as earth represents an infinite charge sink with a theoretical potential of zero.
3. What is the significance of connecting loads in parallel?
Answer: D. It allows independent operations of loads
Rationale: Parallel connections ensure that the same constant voltage is applied across each branch, so turning one load on or off does not interrupt the circuit path for the others.
4. Which of the following safety rules should be observed while working on live electric circuit?
Answer: D. Both A and B
Rationale: Staying insulated from ground prevents a path to earth, and using the one-hand rule prevents a fatal current loop from traveling across your chest (heart).
5. Two ac sources feed a common variable resistive load... Under the maximum power transfer condition, what is the power absorbed by the load resistance R_L?
Answer: C. 625 W
Rationale: Resolving the two sources into a Thevenin equivalent circuit yields V_th = 100 V and R_th = 4 Ω. Maximum power occurs when R_L = R_th (4 Ω). Power = (100 / 8)² × 4 = 625 W.
6. The hot resistance of an incandescent lamp is about ___ times its cold resistance.
Answer: A. 10
Rationale: Tungsten filaments have a high positive temperature coefficient. When heated to operating temperature, the resistance typically increases by a factor of 10 to 15.
7. If a resistor in a series circuit is shorted, the series current, I
Answer: C. increases
Rationale: A short circuit creates a 0 Ω path bypassing the resistor. This reduces the total equivalent resistance of the series circuit. By Ohm's Law (I = V/R), a lower resistance results in a higher current.
8. The equivalent wye element of 3 equal resistors each equal to R and connected in delta is
Answer: B. R/3
Rationale: The standard Delta-to-Wye conversion formula for equal resistors is R_Y = R_Delta / 3.
9. A coil of aluminum wire has a resistance of 50 Ω when its temperature is 0°C. Determine its resistance at 100°C if the temperature coefficient of resistance of aluminum at 0°C is 0.0038/°C.
Answer: A. 69 Ω
Rationale: R_t = R_0(1 + αΔT). R_100 = 50(1 + 0.0038 × 100) = 50(1 + 0.38) = 50(1.38) = 69 Ω.
10. A certain factory takes 250 A at 220 V. It is supplied through a pair of feeders 73.2 meters long and 1.29 cm² in cross section. What voltage must be maintained at the supply end of the feeders?
Answer: B. 222.5 V
Rationale: Calculate total resistance of the lines using length and cross-section, which yields a voltage drop (I×R) of approximately 2.5 V. Add this drop to the 220 V required at the load to get 222.5 V supply.
11. A wire with a smaller cross-sectional area will produce:
Answer: D. more heat
Rationale: A smaller cross-section increases electrical resistance. Higher resistance dissipates more power as heat (P = I²R).
12. Halving the cross-sectional area of a conductor will:
Answer: D. Double the resistance
Rationale: Resistance is inversely proportional to cross-sectional area (R = ρL/A). If A becomes A/2, R becomes 2R.
13. An aluminum wire has a resistance of 50 Ω at 20°C. The wire is heated to a temperature of 100°C. Determine the resistance of the wire at 100°C, assuming that the temperature coefficient of resistance at 0°C is 0.004/°C.
Answer: A. 64.81 Ω
Rationale: First, find base resistance at 0°C: R_0 = 50 / (1 + 0.004 × 20) ≈ 46.29 Ω. Then calculate for 100°C: R_100 = 46.29(1 + 0.004 × 100) = 46.29(1.4) = 64.81 Ω.
14. In Norton's Theorem, the short circuit current is obtained by
Answer: B. shorting the load terminals
Rationale: The Norton equivalent current (I_N) is defined as the current that flows through a direct short circuit placed across the specific load terminals being evaluated.
15. Delta to wye or wye to delta transformation technique is applied to a ___ network.
Answer: C. three-terminal
Rationale: Delta (a triangle) and Wye (a Y-shape) configurations inherently connect exactly three nodes or terminals in a circuit.
16. If the diameter of a wire is doubled, its current carrying capacity becomes
Answer: D. four times
Rationale: Cross-sectional area is proportional to the square of the diameter (A = πd²/4). Doubling the diameter quadruples the area, dropping resistance to 1/4 and allowing 4 times the current capacity.
17. If a resistance element is part of two loops, how many voltage drops must be calculated for that component?
Answer: A. Two
Rationale: In mesh analysis, a shared resistor experiences a voltage drop from the first loop's current and an opposing or additive drop from the second loop's current.
18. Find the equivalent resistance of the figure shown below (Two 20Ω parallel, in series with 10Ω, all parallel to 20Ω).
Answer: A. 10 ohms
Rationale: The two 20 Ω resistors in parallel equal 10 Ω. This is in series with the 10 Ω resistor, totaling 20 Ω for that branch. That 20 Ω branch is in parallel with the main 20 Ω resistor, resulting in a total equivalent resistance of 10 Ω.
19. Suppose you double the voltage in a simple dc circuit and cut the resistance in half. The current will become:
Answer: A. Four times as great
Rationale: Initial I = V/R. New I = (2V) / (0.5R) = 4(V/R) = 4I.
20. In a series circuit, the current, I, is
Answer: B. the same everywhere
Rationale: By Kirchhoff's Current Law, charge cannot accumulate in a single path, so current is uniform throughout a series circuit.
21. When a material becomes a superconductor, its resistivity becomes
Answer: B. zero
Rationale: The defining physical characteristic of a superconductor below its critical temperature is the complete elimination of electrical resistance.
22. "The sum of the emf's and potential differences around a closed loop equals zero" is a consequence of:
Answer: C. Conservation of energy
Rationale: Kirchhoff's Voltage Law dictates that total energy gained (sources) and lost (drops) by a charge in a closed loop must net to zero.
23. Two (2) 115-V incandescent lamps A and B are connected in series across a 230-V source. If lamp A is rated 75 watts and lamp B is rated 50 watts, determine the current drawn by the series connection.
Answer: A. 0.52 A
Rationale: Find resistances using R = V²/P. R_A = 115²/75 = 176.3 Ω. R_B = 115²/50 = 264.5 Ω. Total R = 440.8 Ω. I = 230 V / 440.8 Ω ≈ 0.52 A.
24. A network contains linear resistors and ideal voltage sources. If values of all the resistors are doubled, then the voltage across each resistor is
Answer: D. no change
Rationale: Voltage division depends entirely on the ratio of resistances to one another, not their absolute values. If all scale uniformly, the ratios and voltage drops remain identical.
25. Which of the following is neither a basic physical law nor derivable from one?
Answer: A. Ohm's law
Rationale: Ohm's law is an empirical observation describing how certain materials behave, whereas Coulomb's and Kirchhoff's laws are derived from fundamental physical constants (conservation of charge/energy).
26. Which of the following is the poorest conductor of electricity?
Answer: B. Nichrome
Rationale: Aluminum and Copper are excellent conductors. Nichrome is an alloy explicitly designed for high electrical resistance to generate heat.
27. If a resistor in a series circuit becomes open, how much is the voltage across each of the remaining resistors that are still good?
Answer: C. 0 V
Rationale: An open circuit drops total current to zero. With no current flowing, Ohm's law (V=IR) dictates a 0V drop across the remaining intact resistors. The entire source voltage is dropped across the open break.
28. A series circuit in which desired portions of the source voltage may be tapped off for use in equipment.
Answer: D. Voltage divider
Rationale: A voltage divider uses series resistors to proportionately split the total voltage into smaller, tapped segments.
29. If a complex circuit is reduced to an equivalent circuit consisting of a single voltage source in series with a single resistor, this is an example of:
Answer: B. Thevenin's theorem
Rationale: This is the explicit definition of a Thevenin equivalent circuit. (Norton is a current source in parallel).
30. If one branch of a parallel circuit is short-circuited.
Answer: D. Both A and C
Rationale: The current will bypass all other branches (effectively short-circuiting them as well) and travel through the zero-resistance path, pulling massive current that will blow the main line fuse.
31. Twelve 1 Ω resistance are used as edges to form a cube. What is the resistance between two diagonally opposite corners of the cube?
Answer: A. 5/6 Ω
Rationale: Current entering one corner splits uniformly into 3 branches, then splits into 6 edges, then converges back into 3 branches. R_eq = R/3 + R/6 + R/3 = 5R/6. For 1 Ω, it's 5/6 Ω.
32. The polarity of a resistor's voltage drop is determined by
Answer: A. the direction of current through the resistor
Rationale: By convention, current enters a resistor at the positive (+) polarity terminal and exits at the negative (-) polarity terminal.
33. The electric energy required to raise the temperature of a given amount of water is 1000 kWh. If heat losses are 25%, the total heating energy required is
Answer: B. 1333
Rationale: The 1000 kWh is the useful energy, which is only 75% of the total energy due to 25% losses. Total Energy = 1000 / 0.75 ≈ 1333 kWh.
34. The resistance of a 5 m length of wire is 600 Ω. Determine the resistance of an 8 m length of the same wire.
Answer: A. 960 Ω
Rationale: Resistance scales linearly with length. (600 Ω / 5 m) × 8 m = 120 Ω/m × 8 m = 960 Ω.
35. What is expected when two 20 Ω, 1 watt resistor in parallel are used instead of one 10 Ω, 1 watt?
Answer: C. Provides more power
Rationale: Two 20 Ω resistors in parallel provide an equivalent resistance of 10 Ω. However, their power ratings are additive, meaning the combination can safely dissipate 2 Watts instead of 1 Watt.
36. Twelve identical wires of resistance 6 ohms each are arranged to form the edge of a cube. A current of 40 mA is led into the cube at one corner and out at the other diagonally opposite corner. Calculate the potential difference developed.
Answer: A. 0.20 V
Rationale: Equivalent resistance across diagonal is 5/6 of the edge resistance. R_eq = (5/6) × 6 = 5 Ω. Voltage V = I × R = 0.040 A × 5 Ω = 0.20 V.
37. The superposition theorem is used when the circuit contains
Answer: C. number of voltage sources
Rationale: Superposition is a tool explicitly designed for analyzing linear networks containing two or more independent sources by solving for them one at a time.
38. In a normally operating parallel circuit, the individual branch currents are
Answer: A. independent of each other
Rationale: Because each branch is connected directly across the same voltage source, altering resistance in one branch has no effect on the current flowing through parallel branches.
39. What is a closed path made of several branches of the network called?
Answer: D. Loop
Rationale: Any unbroken path that traverses circuit nodes and returns to its starting point without crossing a node twice is defined as a loop.
40. What is expected when two 20 Ω, 1 watt resistor in parallel are used instead of one 10kΩ (typo: meant 10Ω), 1 watt?
Answer: C. Provides more power
Rationale: Duplicate question of #35. Two 1-watt resistors distribute the heat, yielding a 2-watt total capacity.
41. If one branch of a parallel circuit becomes open,
Answer: C. the remaining branch currents do not change in value
Rationale: Removing a parallel branch only affects the total source current. The applied voltage on the remaining branches stays constant, so their currents stay constant.
42. In a parallel circuit, the individual branch currents are
Answer: C. inversely proportional to the branch resistance values
Rationale: By Ohm’s Law (I = V/R), since voltage is constant across parallel branches, current must drop as resistance increases.
43. In any network made up of linear resistances and containing more than one source... the resultant current flowing in any branch is the algebraic sum... if each source was considered separately. What is this theorem?
Answer: C. Superposition theorem
Rationale: This is the textbook definition of the Superposition Theorem.
44. Two resistors of 4 ohms and 6 ohms are connected in parallel. If the total current is 30 amperes, find the current through each.
Answer: B. 18 A and 12 A
Rationale: Use the current divider rule. I_4 = Total Current × [Opposite R / Sum of R] = 30 × (6 / 10) = 18 A. I_6 = 30 × (4 / 10) = 12 A.
45. If an emf in circuit A produces a current in circuit B, then the same emf in circuit B produces the same current in circuit A. This theorem is known as
Answer: C. Reciprocity theorem
Rationale: The Reciprocity theorem states that in a linear, passive network, the positions of an ideal voltage source and an ideal ammeter can be swapped without changing the ammeter reading.
46. A 100-Ω resistor, R1, and a 300-Ω resistor, R2, are in parallel across a dc voltage source. Which resistor dissipates more power?
Answer: C. The 100-Ω resistor
Rationale: In parallel, voltage is identical. Power P = V²/R. Since R is in the denominator, the smaller resistance draws more current and thus dissipates more power.
47. The power transferred from a supply source to a load is at its maximum when the resistance of the load is _____ the internal resistance of the source.
Answer: C. equal to
Rationale: Direct application of Jacobi's Maximum Power Transfer Theorem.
48. What if you have a 12-strand conductor and each strand has a radius (one-half the diameter) of 2/10 inch, what is the square mil area of the conductor?
Answer: A. 1507965 sq. mil
Rationale: Convert radius to mils: 2/10 inch = 0.2 inch = 200 mils. Area of one strand = πr² = π(200)² ≈ 125,663.7 sq. mil. Total area = 12 × 125,663.7 ≈ 1,507,964 sq. mil.
49. A 5-Ω and 10-Ω resistor are connected in series across a dc voltage source. Which resistor will dissipate more power?
Answer: B. the 10-Ω resistor
Rationale: In series, current is identical across components. Power P = I²R. Because the current is squared and constant, the larger resistance dissipates the most power.
50. The first step in Thevenin's theorem is to:
Answer: B. open the load under test
Rationale: To analyze a network's effect on a specific load, the load must first be temporarily removed (creating an open circuit) to calculate the open-circuit voltage (V_th).
